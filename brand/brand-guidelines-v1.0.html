Brand DNA

What Syltra SMART believes, promises, and sounds like — before a single pixel or panel is designed.

Mission

To make intelligent living feel effortless — engineering technology that disappears into the home, so what remains is calm, control, and quiet confidence.

Vision

A world where every home breathes in sync with the people inside it — private, adaptive, and unmistakably well-made, regardless of which protocol happens to be running underneath.

Brand Promise

Syltra SMART will never make your home feel like a demo. It will make it feel like yours — just quieter, calmer, and a little more capable every year.

Brand Story

Syltra SMART was founded on a specific frustration: smart-home products that were smart in the lab and clumsy in the living room — thin plastic, laggy apps, a cloud that goes dark the moment its server does. We build from the GCC outward as an engineering-first company, applying the same anodized aluminum, glass and matte finishes you'd expect from a flagship phone to a light switch. We treat every open standard — Matter, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread, Home Assistant — as a citizen, not a compromise.

Tone of Voice

Confident, exact, unhurried. We describe what a product does before how it feels. Real specifications over adjectives — "47dB, whisper-quiet," never just "super quiet." We do not say revolutionary, game-changing, or disruptive. The spec and the material speak for themselves.

Values

  • 01
    Precision over noise. Every feature earns its place; nothing ships to fill a spec sheet.
  • 02
    Material honesty. Surfaces look like what they are — metal reads as metal, glass as glass.
  • 03
    Privacy by architecture. Intelligence runs at the edge first; the cloud is an option, never a dependency.
  • 04
    Interoperable by conviction. Matter, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread and Home Assistant are equals, not upsells.
  • 05
    Design for a decade. Hardware and firmware are built to be supported, not replaced.

Logo System

One mark, engineered like the products it represents.

The primary lockup pairs the chrome ideogram — two folded planes reading simultaneously as a toggle mid-switch and the initial "S" — with the Syltra wordmark and the tagline "Smart Living. Seamlessly Connected." The verbal brand name, used in full-legal and first-mention contexts, is Syltra SMART®; the visual wordmark stays the shorter Syltra for the same reason "Apple" dropped "Computer" and "Tesla" dropped "Motors" — the category is already implied by the tagline and the product in frame. This is a deliberate architecture decision, not an inconsistency.

Approved renderings

Renderings above are digital previews derived from the master chrome artwork for internal review only. Production silver / black / white cuts should be vector-redrawn from source paths before release to manufacturing, packaging, or signage vendors.

Design Language

Minimal, industrial, geometric — every surface is machined, not decorated.

Geometry
Symmetrical layouts on a consistent grid; forms built from squared arcs rather than pure circles, echoing the logomark's folded planes.
Corner radius
Token scale 2 / 6 / 16px — "machined, not bubbly." 16px is the largest radius used anywhere, including hero cards.
Glass
Frosted overlays (20–40% opacity) reserved for control surfaces — sheets, modals, panel bezels. Never applied over primary reading content.
Metal & matte
Brushed-aluminum gradients and satin clear-coats (30–40 gloss units) stand in for photography where real material shots aren't available. No glossy plastic, anywhere.
Lighting
One edge-lit accent per surface. Ambient CCT 2700K for lifestyle contexts; Ion blue reserved strictly for status, never for ambience.

Icon System

Line by default. Filled only to mark selection.

Grid
24px keyline grid, 2px internal padding.
Stroke
1.5px line icons for navigation and system states.
Filled variant
Reserved for active/selected states and product-category icons only — never both styles in one row.
Corner radius
4px, matched to the UI's --r-md token so icon and container feel machined from the same block.
Color
Single color (Chrome or Platinum). Ion is permitted only on status icons — a lock engaged, a sensor triggered.
07

Photography Style

One key light, real materials, no posed hands.

Lighting
Single controlled softbox key with a subtle rim; no lens flares, no bokeh text overlays.
Product shots
Pure Void or Platinum sweep backgrounds only — the product is the only subject in frame.
Lifestyle context
Real materials — travertine, oak, brushed concrete — evoking modern Gulf villas and apartments, not stock-photo staging.
Moment
Always "in use" — a hand mid-gesture on a switch — never a posed, static product-in-hand shot.
Grade
Slightly cooled shadows, neutral midtones. Skin tones stay neutral; Ion never tints them.
08

Product Design Language

One vocabulary across hub, switch, lock, sensor and panel — so the range reads as a range.

Finishes
Two only at launch: matte black and matte white anodized aluminum. No third color, no gloss option.
Status light
A single hairline Ion edge-lit ring or line — the one indicator language shared by every device in the line.
Glass
Reserved for touch-panel fronts only, Gorilla-Glass-class with an oleophobic coating.
Fasteners
Hidden on every user-facing surface — no visible screws from normal viewing distance.
Geometry
6mm physical corner radius, the hardware analog of the 16px digital cap.
Branding
One wordmark maximum per device, laser-etched (never printed) on the underside only. Devices should look unbranded from across the room — that restraint is itself the signal.

Packaging Design

The box stays clean enough to keep.

Exterior
Matte black soft-touch laminate box, single chrome S-mark centered on the face. No other copy printed on the box itself.
Specs & copy
Carried entirely on a removable belly band, so the reusable box underneath stays unmarked.
Interior
Molded black recycled-pulp tray — no foam. Product sits face-down under a printed reveal card for a deliberate lift-the-lid moment.
Accessories
Cables and mounts in a flat pouch beneath the tray.
Manual
Replaced entirely by a QR code linking to the setup flow in the Syltra Mobile App.

Retail Store Concept — "The Panel"

A living room you can walk into, not a shelf you browse.

Exterior
Blackened-aluminum storefront, one backlit S-mark. No pricing or signage visible from the street — the space invites rather than sells from outside.
Interior
Travertine or polished-concrete floor; oak product tables at seated height, deliberately "living room," not "shelf."
Experience zone
One full wall built as a miniature apartment — kitchen and living corners running real, live automations.
Lighting
2700K warm ambient throughout, 4000K cool accent reserved for product tables only.
Digital displays
Recessed flush portrait OLED panels looping silent, captioned product films.
Interaction
One touch panel per table triggers the actual automation tied to that product — dim the lights, close the blinds — never a video standing in for the real thing.

Website Design

The system this book describes, applied to syntrasmart.com.

Landing page
Hero and product story alternate down the page, matching this book's own chapter rhythm.
Navigation
Four items maximum — Products / Solutions / Support / Company. Transparent over the hero, solidifies to Void on scroll.

Task-based search ("pair a switch"), not a numbered FAQ list.

Mobile App Design

Rooms first. Devices second.

Dashboard
A rooms-first grid, not a device list, topped by one glanceable energy figure.
Rooms
One card per room showing live thumbnail state.
Automation
Visual flow builder — trigger → condition → action — labeled in mono type.
Scenes
Named presets, each with a single Ion-lit icon.
Security
Always renders on Void regardless of system theme — a deliberate "vault" moment. Cameras and locks grouped above sensors.
Energy
One hero sparkline; kWh translated into local-currency cost, not left as a raw unit.
Devices
Full inventory, filterable by protocol badge — Matter, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread, Wi-Fi, BLE.
Notifications
Grouped by room, not by time.
AI Assistant
Text-first, not a floating orb. Answers link directly to the relevant control, not only to a chat reply.

Industrial Design Guidelines

What's inside the enclosure matters as much as the finish on it.

Materials
6000-series anodized aluminum chassis; Gorilla-Glass-class fronts on panels; glass-filled nylon internally, only where RF needs to pass through.
Manufacturing
CNC-milled aluminum for every visible frame; injection-molded polycarbonate for the internal chassis — a mixed-material approach that protects the touch surface without inflating cost.
Surface finish
Bead-blasted then anodized matte, Ra 0.8–1.6µm target roughness.
LED placement
Always edge-lit through a light-guide film — never a visible bare LED die.
Ventilation
Laser-cut micro-perforation hidden in the base ring, positioned away from any user-facing surface.
Speaker
Micro-drilled grille concealed under the base — no visible mesh on top.
Cooling
Passive only below 3W continuous draw — no fans anywhere in the current line.
Antennas
Routed through the non-metal base ring to avoid Faraday-caging the aluminum shell.

Marketing Guidelines

The spec is the headline.

Social
Product-in-context stills and 6–9s silent, captioned clips. No meme formats, no countdown-timer urgency copy.
Advertising
Spec-led headlines — "47dB. 0.3s response. One app." — over lifestyle taglines.
Billboards
Mark plus one spec, nothing else, always on Void.
Launch events
Small seated demo tables mirroring "The Panel," capped attendance, real installed automations — never a stage keynote or slideware.
Motion graphics
Ion appears only on real state-changes — a light turning on, a lock engaging — never as decorative motion. Hard cuts or one consistent wipe; no cross-dissolves.

Brand Book

This document, and how it should be used.

Syltra SMART Brand Guidelines, v1.0 is the single source of truth for the identity, superseding any earlier exploration. It governs hardware, software, packaging, retail, and marketing without exception.

Any deviation from a rule in this book requires sign-off that names the specific section it departs from — a change to the finish palette in Section 08, for instance, is a Section 08 exception, not a general license. Chapters 11 and 12 describe the website and app at the level this book operates at; the Syltra SMART website currently in development is where those two chapters get built out in full.
