Overview

What this book covers, and where its facts come from.

Why this book exists

SYNTRA is one ecosystem told through two products — SYNTRA TV and SYNTRA Smart Home — sharing one hub, one account, and one intelligence layer called SYNA. This book documents the identity of the Smart Home side only: the dashboard, the physical Hub, the wall-mounted control panel, and SYNA's voice presence.

Every fact, tagline, screen, and color in this book is drawn directly from SYNTRA's own shipped marketing materials and product prompts — the official one-pager carrying the SYNTRA® mark, the SYNA assistant sheet, and the live dashboard and control-panel screens. Nothing here is a hypothetical brand exercise; it is a record of decisions SYNTRA has already made, organized so every future designer, developer, and writer makes the same ones the same way.

What changed to get here

SYNTRA Smart Home's accent color is blue, confirmed against the official marketing one-pager. Green appears nowhere in this identity as a brand color — it is reserved, in the product itself, for one meaning only: a device or hub that is online and connected. That distinction runs through this entire book.

How to use this book

Designers
Chapters 04–06 for color, type, and component patterns; Chapter 09 for dashboard-specific UI rules.
Developers
Chapter 04 for exact hex tokens; Chapter 08 for hardware naming and iconography that must match on-device labels.

Anyone writing for SYNA

Brand DNA

Mission, values, and tone — as evidenced by what SYNTRA has already shipped.

Mission

A unified smart-home control platform integrated with SYNTRA TV — one interface for monitoring, automation, security, and entertainment.

Source: SYNTRA Home Assistant PRD, Vision statement.

Brand line

تلفزيون أذكى. منزل أذكى. حياة أسهل.

Smarter TV. Smarter Home. Simpler Life.

كل الترفيه. كل التحكم. تجربة واحدة متكاملة.

Entertainment like never before. Control everything effortlessly.

Source: official SYNTRA marketing one-pager.

Values, as built

These are not aspirational statements — each is a rule already enforced somewhere in the SYNTRA product or its build prompts.

  • 01
    One interface, not many apps
    The build rule is explicit: never expose the default Home Assistant UI. Every device, camera, and scene surfaces through one custom SYNTRA interface.
  • 02
    Bilingual by default, not by translation
    Every shipped screen and every piece of marketing copy exists natively in Arabic and English side by side — not one localized from the other.
  • 03
    The hardware disappears
    The SYNTRA Hub is marketed as “the brain of your smart home,” not as a gadget. Home Assistant itself is described as running quietly in the background.
  • 04
    Privacy is a default, not a setting
    “Your privacy first” and “your data is fully protected and shared with no one” are load-bearing claims on SYNTRA's own marketing sheet, not fine print.
  • 05
    Everything talks to everything
    SYNTRA TV sits in the Smart Home dashboard's bottom navigation. The Home Assistant build prompt requires direct integration with SYNTRA TV and SYNTRA Cloud.
  • 06
    Calm intelligence, not noise
    SYNA is described as “smart and proactive” — it learns and suggests. Scenes like Sleep Mode and Away Mode do several things from one tap instead of asking for several.

Tone of voice

Observed in shipped copy
  • Short, paired headlines: a bold Arabic line with a lighter English line underneath, or vice versa.
  • Direct address and personalization — the dashboard greets “مرحباً انس” (Welcome, Anas) by name.
  • Plain claims stated once, without exclamation marks: “4K Ultra HD,” “fully protected,” “runs in the background.”
  • Voice commands are addressed to SYNA by name first: “Syna, show the cameras.”
Not the SYNTRA voice
  • Hype language, exclamation points, or superlatives not tied to a spec (no shipped copy uses them).
  • English-only or Arabic-only messaging — every real example pairs both.
  • Referring to the Hub or panel as a gadget or device rather than as the home's control layer.

Market

SYNTRA Smart Home is built Arabic-first for Saudi Arabia and the GCC — the dashboard defaults to Arabic, weather and locale data are localized to Riyadh, and the TV product leads with Arabic and international channel availability. English is a fully supported second surface, not an afterthought.

Logo System

Three marks, one ecosystem: SYNTRA HOME, SYNA, and the shared SYNTRA wordmark.

SYNTRA HOME — the product lockup

The official mark for this product line: a house-roof outline in gradient blue wrapping the SYNTRA swoosh icon, with a small blue window-grid glyph beneath it — paired with the “SYNTRA” wordmark in chrome and “HOME” set in the brand's blue accent, under the locked tagline “Smart Living. Seamlessly Connected.”

This is the primary lockup for everything documented in this book. The house-roof glyph is unique to the Home product — it does not appear on the SYNTRA TV or bare SYNTRA marks — and “HOME” is the one place in the entire wordmark system where the product word breaks from chrome into full accent-blue, making it the clearest single signal that a surface belongs to the Smart Home product line.

SYNTRA wordmark (shared)

The bare lockup used where no product suffix applies: the diagonal swoosh mark, the SYNTRA® wordmark, and the locked tagline, set between two hairline rules.

The SYNTRA mark is a chrome/white wordmark set in a bold, geometric sans, always paired with the registered mark and, in full lockups, the tagline. It sits on dark navy/void backgrounds in every observed application.

Icon mark

The bare icon-only mark for favicons and the physical Hub. The Home product uses its own variant of this icon (above) with the added roof glyph and window grid.

SYNA — the assistant mark

SYNA's wordmark uses a blue-to-white gradient, distinguishing it from the solid-chrome SYNTRA wordmark, under the line “The Intelligence Behind SYNTRA” / “سينا، العقل الذكي لمنظومة سينترا.”

SYNA is a sub-brand, not a competing identity: it is always introduced as running inside or alongside SYNTRA, never on its own. Its icon is an abstract glowing orb with a swoosh at its center — the same swoosh gesture as the SYNTRA mark, rendered as light rather than as a solid mark.

SYNTRA TV — the sibling lockup, for reference

Out of scope for this book (see Chapter 01), shown only to establish the shared lockup pattern: SYNTRA's product marks pair the chrome wordmark with a product word and a locked tagline — here “TV” stays chrome/monochrome, next to “ENTERTAINMENT. ANYTIME. ANYWHERE.”

Seeing both official product lockups side by side confirms the rule in Chapter 04: TV's product word carries no accent color at all, while HOME's does — blue is specifically the Smart Home product's signal, not a general SYNTRA accent applied everywhere.

Do
  • Use the SYNTRA HOME lockup (with the roof-glyph icon) for anything representing this product; use the bare SYNTRA mark only where no product line is implied.
  • Keep every mark on dark backgrounds; use the chrome/white lockups, not recolored variants.
  • Let SYNA's mark glow — it is built from radial light, not a flat icon.
Don't
  • Recolor the SYNTRA swoosh green — it has never shipped in that color, on Home, TV, or the bare mark.
  • Use the plain SYNTRA icon where the Home-specific roof-glyph icon is called for, or vice versa.
  • Present SYNA's wordmark in flat solid blue; the gradient is part of the mark.

05

Typography

One typeface, built to carry Arabic and Latin scripts at equal weight.

Typeface

Cairo — a variable typeface spanning weights 200 to 1000, with matched Arabic and Latin character sets in the same font file.

Every shipped SYNTRA surface pairs Arabic and English in the same layout, at the same visual weight — never a heavier display face for one language and a fallback system font for the other. Cairo was selected for this book because it is one of the few production-grade variable fonts that natively covers both scripts without a visual mismatch in x-height or stroke weight.

Weight roles

Display · 800
Cover titles, chapter titles, headline pairs like “SYNTRA TV” / “كل الترفيه.”
Emphasis · 600
Card titles, device names, nav labels — “Living Room Lights,” “غرفة النوم.”
Body · 400–500
Descriptions, captions, supporting copy in both languages.
Utility · 700, tracked
Eyebrows, chapter numbers, status labels, spec badges (“4K ULTRA HD,” “HDR 10+”).

Bilingual pairing pattern

The recurring pattern across every real screen is a bold Arabic (or English) headline directly followed by a lighter-weight line in the other language — never a full paragraph translation, always a short restatement. For example: “تلفزيون أذكى. منزل أذكى. حياة أسهل.” followed by “Smarter TV. Smarter Home. Simpler Life.” Preserve this rhythm rather than running parallel columns of full-length translated text.

Do
  • Set Arabic right-to-left with native Arabic-Indic or Western numerals matching the surrounding UI convention (SYNTRA's shipped screens use Western numerals: “23°C,” “2.45 kW”).
  • Keep both languages in Cairo at matching weights.
Don't
  • Mix in a second Arabic typeface for “flavor” — every shipped surface uses one face for both scripts.
  • Justify Arabic body text; SYNTRA's real screens are left/right-aligned by direction, never justified.
06

Design Language & Iconography

The card-and-toggle vocabulary that makes every device feel like the same product.

The card

Every controllable thing in SYNTRA Smart Home — a light, a lock, an AC unit, a leak sensor — is represented the same way: a rounded, dark surface card with a line icon, a name, a live value, and (where relevant) a toggle or stepper. This holds true whether the card lives on the phone dashboard or the wall-mounted panel.

Recurring components, as observed

Room map
An isometric 3D floor plan with tappable room pins showing live temperature and status — with an explicit 2D/3D toggle.
Favorite device grid
A 2-column card grid of the household's most-used controls, each with an on/off toggle and, where relevant, a percentage slider.
Scenario / scene list
A vertical list of named automations (not raw device lists) — each with an icon, a name, a one-line description of what it does, and a single play control.
Quick shortcuts
A small grid of instant, no-confirmation actions — “turn off all lights,” “lock all doors” — separate from the scenario list.
Notification feed
A reverse-chronological list, each item carrying a colored status icon (lock, motion, consumption) and a relative timestamp (“since 2 minutes”).
Bottom tab bar
Home, SYNTRA TV, a centered raised microphone button for SYNA, Notifications, Settings — the entertainment product and the voice assistant both live in primary navigation, not buried in settings.

Iconography style

Icons throughout are thin-line, single-weight, and monochrome (white or slate on dark, blue only when active/selected) — lighting, climate, curtains, locks, cameras, and energy each get one consistent glyph reused identically across the dashboard, the control panel, and marketing material.

Do
  • Give every automation a name and a one-line plain-language description, not just a device list.
  • Use a single accent-blue highlight for the one active/selected state per view.
Don't
  • Introduce filled, two-tone, or illustrative icon styles alongside the thin-line set.
  • Show raw device toggles without grouping them into rooms or favorites — the product never presents an ungrouped device list as the primary view.

Wake word & command grammar

Every real command begins by addressing SYNA by name, then states the action plainly — no filler words, no questions back and forth for simple actions:

“سينا، اعرض الكاميرات” — Syna, show the cameras “سينا، أطفئ جميع الأنوار” — Syna, turn off all the lights “سينا، افتح الستائر” — Syna, open the curtains “سينا، ارفع الكيف 22” — Syna, set the AC to 22

The on-screen assistant panel greets with: “سينا — مرحباً، أنا سينا. كيف أستطيع مساعدتك؟” (“Syna — Hello, I'm Syna. How can I help?”) alongside a live waveform and a set of one-tap quick actions (Cinema Mode, Lights Off, Show Cameras, AC to 22, Play Music) — SYNA offers shortcuts, it doesn't only wait for open-ended speech.

SYNA's visual signature

SYNA is never shown as a static icon in its own promotional material — it is a glowing blue orb with a radiating waveform ring, the same swoosh gesture as the SYNTRA mark rendered as light. On-screen, it always appears with a live audio waveform, reinforcing that it is a voice-first, not a chat-first, presence.

Privacy is part of SYNA's introduction, not a footnote. Its own marketing sheet leads with “خصوصيتك أولا” / “Your privacy first” — data fully protected and shared with no one — directly alongside its capability claims.

Product & Hardware Design Language

Two physical touchpoints: the Hub, and the 10.1" control screen.

SYNTRA Hub (foreground, TV console) and the 10.1" wall-mounted control screen, shown in situ with the ambient blue backlight both devices share.

SYNTRA Hub

“The brain of your smart home & entertainment.”

A small, black, cylindrical/cube-form puck with a single thin blue light strip — its only visible UI is that light. It sits below or beside the TV, deliberately unobtrusive, reinforcing that the intelligence lives in the network, not in a screen you look at directly.

10.1" Control Screen

“Smart home control at your fingertips.”

A wall-mounted panel, black bezel, matching the TV and Hub's dark material language. Its home screen is the same card-and-scene vocabulary as the phone dashboard (Chapter 06) — the same information architecture regardless of which physical surface it's rendered on: Home, Lighting, Climate, Security, Cameras, Shades, Energy, Scenes, Settings, with a persistent voice bar for SYNA at the bottom.

Material & environment cues

Ambient lighting
Every hardware photograph shows the product environment lit in blue, not neutral white — the accent color extends into the physical space, not just the screen.
Placement
Hub: low, near the TV console. Panel: wall-mounted at a glance-and-tap height, styled like a smart thermostat rather than a tablet bolted to the wall.
Labeling
Product photography labels each device in plain English captions (“SYNTRA HUB,” “10.1" CONTROL SCREEN”) directly under its one-line role description — keep this pairing in any future hardware marketing.

App & Dashboard UI

The Smart Home dashboard, screen by screen.

The live Smart Home dashboard (Arabic), showing the greeting header, stat chips, 3D room map, favorites grid, scenarios, and notifications.

Page anatomy, top to bottom

Header
Logo top-left; a personalized greeting (“مرحباً انس” — Welcome, Anas) and a one-line status summary (“كل شيء تحت السيطرة” — everything under control); search, messages, and notification icons top-right.
Stat chips
A row of four whole-home vitals: temperature, air quality, power consumption, and doors/windows status — always visible, never nested in a menu.
Home map
An isometric 3D floor plan as the visual centerpiece, with a 2D/3D toggle and tappable room pins carrying live values.
Favorites grid
User-curated device cards with inline toggles and sliders — the most-used controls, not an alphabetical device list.
Right rail
Scenarios (named automations with a one-tap play control) above Notifications (timestamped event feed) above Quick Shortcuts (instant global actions).
Bottom nav
Home, SYNTRA TV, a raised center microphone button for SYNA, Notifications, Settings.

Localization notes

The dashboard's Arabic build and the control panel's English build carry the same structure and near-identical labels (السيناريوهات / Scenes, الإشعارات / Notifications, الأجهزة / Devices), confirming the information architecture is designed once and localized, not redesigned per language. Numerals, temperatures, and units stay in Western format in both languages.

The one status color on this screen is green — a small dot next to “SYNTRA Hub · متصل” (Connected) in the sidebar. It is the only non-blue, non-neutral color on the entire dashboard, which is exactly its job: to be the one thing that's a different color because it's reporting a different kind of information (system health, not brand).

Marketing & Messaging

How SYNTRA has actually talked about itself.

Feature claims, always paired with proof

Every capability claim in SYNTRA's marketing is stated once, plainly, and immediately backed by a concrete detail — never a claim left to float on its own:

Claim (EN)Claim (AR)Proof point
Your home within reachالمنزل في متناولكFull control of smart devices from the TV screen
Cinematic qualityجودة سينمائية4K Ultra HD · HDR10+, immersive surround sound
Smart voice assistantمساعد صوتي ذكيNatural voice control in Arabic and English
Privacy & securityخصوصية وأمانData fully protected, complete privacy control
A world of contentعالم من المحتوىMovies, series, live channels, and favorite apps in one place

Spec badges

Technical credibility is signaled with small bordered badges rather than prose — 4K ULTRA HD, HDR 10+, Dolby Vision·Atmos, Wi-Fi 6. Reuse this badge pattern for any future spec-level claim rather than writing specs into body copy.

The closing line pattern

Marketing pieces close with a short bilingual banner naming everything the ecosystem does in one breath — “كل ما تحبه.. في مكان واحد” / “Everything you love.. in one place” — followed by a row of six pillar icons: Live, Movies & Series, Games, Apps, Smart Home, and AI. Smart Home is always listed as a first-class pillar of the entertainment product, never a separate pitch.

Do
  • Pair every claim with one concrete, checkable detail.
  • Use the six-pillar closing pattern (Live, Movies & Series, Games, Apps, Smart Home, AI) when summarizing the full ecosystem.
Don't
  • Make unproven superlative claims (“the best,” “the world's most”) — none appear in shipped copy.
  • Pitch Smart Home as a separate product from SYNTRA TV in customer-facing marketing; they are shown as one experience.
